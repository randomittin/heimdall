## 1. Setup and methodology

The current committed snapshot (`generated_at: 2026-09-01T17:51:53.571277+00:00`) was backed
up to a scratch path before running `refresh`, so a per-prompt before/after comparison is
possible (the refresh overwrites `evals/caveman/snapshot.json` in place). "Before" = header-ban
rules (pre-`13678a6`). "After" = consolidated rules (post-`13678a6`, what is live today —
confirmed `bin/heimdall-caveman rules ultra | wc -c` → 2668 bytes, matching the consolidation
doc exactly).

Two kinds of comparison, both needed and easy to conflate:

- **Raw (unpaired) median/mean** — median/mean of `output_tokens` for one arm's 30 responses,
  independent of any other arm. Answers "how many tokens does this arm use, absolutely."
- **Paired median/mean** — `bin/heimdall-caveman-eval`'s own formula
  (`bin/heimdall-caveman-eval:464,479-480`): `savings_i = 1 - (level_i / reference_i)` per
  prompt, then `statistics.median/mean/stdev` (sample stdev) over the 30 per-prompt values.
  Answers "for the same prompt, how much better/worse is level vs reference." This is what
  `bin/heimdall-caveman-eval report`'s own median/mean columns already are — reused directly,
  not reimplemented.
- **tokens-per-visible-word** — `output_tokens / len(text.split())`, mean of the per-prompt
  ratio across 30 prompts (whitespace word count, no markdown stripping — consistent with the
  "words" column methodology in `docs/analysis/2026-09-01-caveman-ultra-parity-research.md`
  §3). Not produced by the harness; computed ad hoc, same as it was originally in the
  2026-08-30/2026-09-02 docs.
- **Sign test** — per prompt, does `after` `output_tokens` exceed `before`? Two-sided
  `scipy.stats.binomtest(decreases, n_effective, 0.5)`. This is §8's own method
  (`docs/analysis/2026-08-30-caveman-eval-measurement.md`), applied here to the header-ban→
  consolidation transition instead of the no-ladder→ladder transition it originally tested.

**Methodology validated before trusting it on new data**: run cold against the *before*
snapshot alone and checked against every number already on record in this brief and in §9 of
the measurement doc — all matched exactly:

hmd_ultra's ratio **did fall**, in the predicted direction. But two things undercut treating
this as confirmation of "constraint count drives the hidden-token cost":

1. **It didn't reach the predicted range.** The consolidation doc's prediction was "3.54 toward
   a lower range (roughly 2.9–3.2)". Measured: 3.30 — outside the range, on the high side, about
   30% of the way short of even the range's own upper edge relative to the full predicted span.
2. **The fall is smaller than this run's own noise floor.** `__baseline__` — an arm with *no
   system prompt whatsoever*, structurally incapable of being affected by any rule change —
   drifted **+0.2551** this run, larger in magnitude than hmd_ultra's own **−0.2401** fall, in
   the opposite direction. `hmd_lite`, also untouched, drifted −0.1138 in the *same* direction
   as hmd_ultra, at about half the magnitude. Given an inert control can move further than
   hmd_ultra moved, hmd_ultra's fall is not distinguishable from resampling noise at this n.

## 6. Sign test: before vs after, per arm (n=30, paired by prompt)

Method: for each prompt, does `after.output_tokens` exceed `before.output_tokens`? Two-sided
`scipy.stats.binomtest(decreases, n_effective, 0.5)`. This is §8's own method
(`docs/analysis/2026-08-30-caveman-eval-measurement.md`), the one that correctly rejected the
intensity-ladder hypothesis; untouched-arm p-values are the noise floor.

| arm | rules touched? | increases | decreases | ties | p (two-sided) |
|---|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| **hmd_ultra** | **yes** | 16 | 14 | 0 | **0.8555** |
| __baseline__ | no | 22 | 8 | 0 | **0.0161** |
| upstream_skill | no | 19 | 11 | 0 | 0.2005 |
| hmd_lite | no | 12 | 18 | 0 | 0.3616 |
| hmd_full | no | 13 | 17 | 0 | 0.5847 |
| __terse__ | no | 14 | 15 | 1 | 1.0000 |

**hmd_ultra itself (p=0.8555) shows the least evidence of any systematic before/after shift of
all six arms** — essentially a coin flip, consistent with "no regression, no detected uniform
improvement either" in raw per-prompt token counts.

**The load-bearing caveat**: `__baseline__` — the one arm that is *definitionally* incapable of
being affected by any rule-text change — is the single most "significant" result in the whole
table (p=0.0161, conventionally significant at α=0.05). This is not evidence of a real effect;
it is direct, in-run proof that this harness's noise floor alone can cross p<0.05 on an
untouched arm, most likely explained by the CLI-version confound (§2). It is the reason every
p-value in this table, including hmd_ultra's own, must be read relative to the other five rows,
never in isolation.

## 7. Prediction verdicts

The consolidation agent's falsifiable prediction
(`docs/analysis/2026-09-02-caveman-ultra-consolidation.md` §4): *"tokens-per-visible-word falls
3.54 → ~2.9–3.2 while median-tokens-vs-upstream holds ≥ +8.5%."*

- **Half A — ratio falls to ~2.9–3.2: REFUTED.** It fell (3.544 → 3.304), matching the
  predicted *direction*, but landed outside the predicted range, and the fall's magnitude
  (−0.240) is smaller than the noise-floor drift measured on the untouched `__baseline__`
  control this same run (+0.255, opposite direction — see §5). A real, right-direction move
  that cannot be distinguished from resampling noise at n=30 does not meet the bar the
  prediction set. Note the softer, explicitly-stated fallback threshold in the same doc ("if the
  ratio stays ≥~3.5, that falsifies...") is technically not triggered either (3.30 < 3.5) — the
  honest read is that this measurement is genuinely inconclusive on the causal question, tilted
  toward refuted because it missed the specific target range and the effect size is
  noise-floor-sized, not because the ratio moved the wrong way.
- **Half B — median holds ≥ +8.5%: CONFIRMED.** +12.11%, an improvement over +8.5%, not merely
  a hold. Unambiguous on its own criterion. (§3's caveat still applies to *why* it improved —
  see above.)

**Combined**: the prediction was stated as a conjunction ("that combination would support the
hypothesis"). Half B holds cleanly; Half A does not clear its bar. The combined result does
**not** provide confident support for "constraint count (not clause wording) drives the
hidden-token cost" — consistent with the doc's own stated fallback interpretation, that if the
ratio doesn't clearly fall into range, the more likely explanation is one specific clause (the
per-item-example ban) rather than aggregate constraint count. This measurement neither proves
nor rules that fallback out; it just fails to confirm the count-based mechanism cleanly.

## 8. Regression check and recommendation

**No regression.** Every headline hmd_ultra number vs the header-ban baseline held or improved:
paired median vs upstream +8.5%→+12.1%, paired mean −4.0%→+6.9%, paired stdev 49.4%→35.3%
(tighter), vs-terse roughly flat-to-better with a tighter stdev, before/after sign test shows no
detectable systematic shift in either direction (p=0.86, the least significant of all six arms).
The tokens-per-visible-word ratio moved in the *safe* direction (down, not up) even though it
didn't clear the predicted range.

**Recommendation: KEEP.** The consolidation delivered its stated structural goal (34→27 literal
clauses, 2990→2668 bytes, §1/§2 of the consolidation doc) with zero measured downside on this
run: no metric got worse, several improved, and the one metric that didn't hit its specific
predicted target (tokens-per-visible-word range) still moved in the right direction and the
underlying causal hypothesis it was testing is left genuinely open rather than disproven. This
is a judgment call for the owner to override, not a unilateral revert — reasoning stated in full
above for that purpose.
